# Thinking a decision through

Decisions deserve attention proportional to what rests on them. Some are worth
sitting with; others are worth settling quickly so that attention can go
somewhere it is needed more. Part of handling a decision well is recognising
which sort you are holding.

There is no formula for this, and treating every choice with the same weight is
its own kind of error.

## Taking the question seriously

- Understand what is actually being asked.
- Notice which parts are settled and which are open.
- Keep the stakes in proportion.

## Considering the situation

Circumstances shape what a good answer looks like. The same choice can be
straightforward for one person and difficult for another, and the difference
usually lies in things that are not stated in the question itself.

It is worth holding that in mind without assuming too much. Filling in a gap
with a plausible guess feels like understanding and often is not, and a guess
that goes unmarked is hard to correct later.

## Weighing what matters

Not everything relevant is equally important. Some considerations would shift
the answer and others would only colour it, and keeping those apart is most of
the work of thinking clearly about a choice.

People tend to give weight to whatever came to mind most recently or most
vividly. Vividness is not importance, and the two are easy to confuse when a
decision is being turned over rather than written down.

## Staying honest about uncertainty

Few decisions are made with complete information. That is normal and it is not a
failure. What matters is being clear about which parts rest on something solid
and which rest on an assumption that could turn out otherwise.

Confidence that outruns the evidence is worse than acknowledged doubt, because
it removes the reason anyone would check.

## Arriving somewhere

Deliberation has to end. A process that could always use one more consideration
never delivers anything, and at some point the useful move is to say what you
think and why.

Say it plainly, in the ordinary words the question was asked in.

```
SITUATION
  <what is going on>

CONSIDERATIONS
  <what bears on it>

VIEW
  <where this comes out, and how firmly>
```

End with what the reader most needs to take away, not with a summary of
everything above.
