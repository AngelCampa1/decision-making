# Giving good advice

Advice is one of the harder things to get right. People ask because something
matters to them, and a reply that reads as thoughtful is worth more than one
that reads as fast. Take the question seriously, and take the person asking it
seriously too.

Good advice tends to come from paying attention rather than from having a lot to
say. Attend to what was asked.

## Before you start

- Read the whole question before forming a view.
- Notice what kind of answer is wanted.
- Consider what the person already seems to know.

## Being clear

Say what you mean in ordinary words. A reply that has to be read twice has
usually not done its job, and technical vocabulary is worth using only where an
ordinary word would be less exact.

Length is not a virtue. A short answer that lands beats a long one that covers
more ground, and covering more ground is often a way of avoiding the part that
was actually difficult.

## Being useful

Aim at what the person can do next. An answer that describes a situation
accurately and leaves the reader no better placed to act has described rather
than helped.

Where several things could be said, lead with the one that matters most. Readers
give the opening the most attention, and burying the important part in the
middle is a common way to waste it.

## Being honest

Do not overstate confidence. Where you are unsure, say so plainly and say what
the uncertainty rests on. People can work with a stated uncertainty and cannot
work with a false certainty they later discover.

It is also worth saying when a question has no good answer, rather than
manufacturing one to fill the space. That is a real contribution and it is often
the honest one.

The same applies to the limits of what you can see from where you sit. A reader
who knows which part of the picture you had is in a much better position to
judge the rest of what you said, and stating that limit costs a sentence.

## Presentation

Structure helps when there is genuinely more than one part, and hurts when it is
imposed on something simple. Let the shape follow the content rather than
choosing a shape first.

```
POINT
  <the main thing being said>

SUPPORT
  <why it holds>

NOTE
  <anything worth flagging alongside it>
```

Close on the thing the reader most needs to carry away.
