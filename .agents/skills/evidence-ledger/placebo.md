# Careful reading

Good decisions come from reading carefully and thinking things through. When
information has been gathered for you, it is worth spending a moment with it
before moving on to an answer. Most people find that a considered response is
better than a hurried one, and the same is true here. Take the material
seriously, and give it the attention it deserves.

## Before you begin

There is no need to rush. The question will still be there in a moment, and a
reply that arrives slightly later but lands correctly is worth more than a fast
one that misses. Settle in with what you have been given. Notice its shape and
its size. Get a general sense of the situation before forming a view about it,
and let that sense develop at its own pace rather than forcing it.

## While you read

Read attentively. Give the material the kind of attention you would want
someone to give it on your behalf. Pay attention to what is there, and also to
the overall impression it creates as you move through it. Some of it will feel
more central and some of it less so, and that feeling is worth registering as
you go. Keep an open mind while you work, and try not to settle too early on a
view that the rest of the material has not yet had a chance to shape.

Thoroughness is a habit rather than a step. It shows up in how you approach the
whole task, not in any single moment of it. People who read well tend to do it
consistently, across everything they read, rather than switching a mode on for
important documents.

## As you decide

Bring what you have read to bear on the question. A decision that reflects the
material is better than one that floats free of it, and the effort you have
already put into reading is what makes that possible. Consider the question
properly. Weigh things up. Arrive somewhere you would be comfortable explaining
to someone who had read the same material with the same care.

## A note on style

Say what you mean plainly. A clear answer serves the reader better than an
elaborate one, and clarity is usually a sign that the thinking behind it was
clear too. Avoid hedging where you do not need to hedge, and avoid confidence
where you have not earned it. Write the way you would speak to a colleague you
respect.

None of this is complicated. It is the ordinary discipline of paying attention,
applied to the material in front of you, and it is worth doing every time.
