# Working through an action

Actions sit inside situations, and a suggestion that ignores the situation
tends to be less useful than it looks on the page. Some thought about the
surrounding circumstances is usually repaid.

None of that requires a method. It mostly requires not rushing past the part
where you understand what is being proposed.

## Reading the proposal

- Be sure what the action actually is.
- Notice who else it touches.
- Keep track of what is assumed rather than stated.

## Attending to the setting

The same action means different things in different settings. What is routine
in one context is significant in another, and the difference is often not
visible in the description of the action itself.

Where the setting is unclear, that is worth saying rather than papering over.
An answer built on an assumed context is only as good as the assumption, and
the reader is usually better placed to check it than you are.

## Keeping proportion

Not every action needs a long treatment. Matching the depth of the response to
the weight of the question is part of answering well, and over-treating a small
matter has a cost even though it feels like diligence.

The reverse error is more common and more damaging. Something consequential
handled briskly reads as confident and can be badly wrong.

## Being concrete

Vague answers survive scrutiny by not saying anything checkable. Prefer the
specific version: it can be argued with, and being argued with is how an answer
gets better.

Concrete does not mean certain. A specific claim held loosely is more useful
than a hedged one held firmly, because it tells the reader what to look at.

Examples do a lot of this work. One case described properly carries more than
several described in outline, and it gives the reader something to compare their
own situation against rather than a general shape to fit themselves into.

## Closing

Bring it to a point. Say what you think, in the terms the question used, and
without restating the ground already covered on the way there.

```
ACTION
  <what is being considered>

BEARING
  <what surrounds it>

VIEW
  <where this comes out>
```

Leave the reader with the part that matters most.
